第一章 · 河边的灯

末班车驶过桥头时，雨刚停。林照把书从帆布袋里取出来，指尖碰到一角微湿的封面。他没有翻开，只把它往怀里挪了挪。

河对岸的窗子一盏接一盏亮起来。那些光落进水里，被缓慢的波纹推远，又重新聚拢。小时候，他总以为河里也住着一座城，只是那里的人要等天黑才起床。

桥下的小店还开着。老板坐在门口剥一只橘子，看见他，便把旁边的木凳往外推了一点。

“回来啦？”

他点点头。原来有些话，可以隔着很多年，仍旧接得上。

袋里的书是下午在旧书摊买的，纸页边缘已经发软。他坐下，借着门口的灯翻到夹着车票的那一页。票上的日期很淡，字迹却还清楚。

老板没有问他要去哪儿，只递来一半橘子。潮湿的空气里忽然有了很轻的甜味。

月亮从云缝里露出来，照着河面，也照着摊开的书。他慢慢读完一行，又读了下一行。今晚似乎不必急着走。

Chapter One · A Light by the River

The rain had stopped by the time the last bus crossed the bridge. Lin took a book out of his canvas bag. One corner of the cover was damp. He drew it closer without opening it.

Windows lit up across the river, one after another. Their reflections drifted apart on the slow water and gathered again. As a child, he had imagined another town beneath the surface, where everyone waited until dark to wake.

The little shop below the bridge was still open. Its owner sat in the doorway peeling an orange. When he saw Lin, he pushed a wooden stool toward the street.

“Back again?”

Lin nodded. Some conversations, it seemed, could resume after years without anyone needing to explain.

He had found the book at a secondhand stall that afternoon. Its pages had softened at the edges. Under the doorway light, he opened it where an old bus ticket had been left. The date was faint, but still readable.

The shopkeeper did not ask where he was going. He simply handed him half the orange. A little sweetness entered the damp air.

The moon appeared between the clouds, lighting the river and the open book. Lin read a line, then another. There was no need to hurry tonight.
